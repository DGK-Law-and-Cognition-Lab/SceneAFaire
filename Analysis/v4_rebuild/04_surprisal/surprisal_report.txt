LENGTH CONFOUND CHECK
======================================================================
  Pearson r(surprisal, log_word_count) = -0.522
  Pearson r(stockiness, log_word_count) = -0.266
  Both measures are correlated with plot length in the same direction,

======================================================================
HEADLINE: corpus-wide Pearson r between mean surprisal and mean per-film
narrative stockiness, with and without length control
======================================================================
  RAW     Pearson r   = +0.005  (n = 45,953, p = 3.102e-01)
  PARTIAL Pearson r   = -0.163  (controlling for log(word_count), p = 3.728e-272)
  Spearman ρ (raw)    = -0.026
  partial R²          = 2.67%  (variance shared after removing length confound)

Interpretation guide (use the PARTIAL r for the doctrinal claim):
  |partial r| < 0.20  weak / no relationship — element-level measures fully independent of plot predictability
  |partial r| 0.20-0.40  modest correlation — directional agreement, framework retains independent doctrinal claim
  |partial r| 0.40-0.60  moderate correlation — framework shares meaningful variance with plot predictability but is not redundant
  |partial r| > 0.60  strong correlation — element-level measures are essentially a re-skinned plot-predictability metric (doctrinal claim weakened)

Expected sign of the partial r: NEGATIVE
  Negative partial r means predictable plots use stock elements and unpredictable
  plots use less-stock elements, which is the doctrinally intuitive direction.
  A positive partial r would be counter-intuitive and worth investigating.
