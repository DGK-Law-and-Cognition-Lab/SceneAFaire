Reading the baselines: across genres both dimensions pick out the court's
genre well above chance, and prevalence does so at least as well as
distinctiveness, so the raw gap above was a difference of scale. Within a
genre, court-named elements exceed matched controls on each dimension with
the other held fixed. The prevalence difference is the larger one in the
full set, but the head-noun null shows that the matching step alone
produces the prevalence of the head-noun matches, while their
distinctiveness is above that null. On exact matches the two differences
are of similar size. The case record therefore supports "high on both"
and does not rank the two dimensions.

Interpretation: judicially identified scenes a faire sit high in their
genre's ranking, well above the random baseline, but that percentile is
descriptive: raw film count reproduces most of it (September 2026 audit).
The primary judicial statistic is genre specificity: the same elements rank
higher in the genre the court assigned than in their other genres. The
baselines above, not the raw gap, bear on which dimension carries it.
Precision@K from Notebook 07 is not reported because it inverts the
inference: the case record is a sparse sample of disputes, not a complete
catalog of stock elements.
